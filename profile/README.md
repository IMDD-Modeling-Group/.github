# Integrated Mechanistic & Data-driven Modeling Group

**Pharmaceutical Informatics · Department of Drug Design and Pharmacology · University of Copenhagen**

We build predictive models of disease progression and drug response by combining mechanistic biological knowledge with data-driven methods. Our aim is reliable precision dosing and virtual-twin prediction in settings where data are scarce and clinical trials have historically under-served patients: pregnancy, liver disease, rare diseases, and obesity.

---

## Our approach

**1. Uncertainty-aware mechanistic modelling.** We use index analysis, model reduction, and identifiability analysis to determine which biological mechanisms the available data can actually support, and to turn large QSP models into ones that can be fitted to clinical data.

**2. Mechanistic models meet machine learning.** We integrate mechanistic models with machine learning and synthetic data, so that we can learn across fragmented datasets instead of building one model per dataset.

**3. Clinical translation.** We start with gestational-age-dependent PBPK and QSP models for dosing in pregnancy, and extend to settings such as obesity through collaborations within the University of Copenhagen and with industry.

**Reusable by the community.** To make methods reusable, we develop community benchmarks and datasets, including through the ISoP AI/ML Special Interest Group.

## What we are working on now

- Model-informed precision dosing during pregnancy, including a first mechanistic pregnancy model
- Curated model repositories and structured physiological datasets
- Model reduction for high-dimensional QSP models
- Model-informed precision dosing in obesity (with industry partners)
- Predictive AI benchmarking in clinical pharmacology
- Virtual patients and in-silico evidence for paediatric rare diseases
- Disease progression modelling in fatty liver disease (MASLD/MASH)

## About the group leader

Jane Knöchel is a Tenure-track Assistant Professor and an applied mathematician by training (Diploma, Humboldt University Berlin; PhD, University of Potsdam, via the PharMetrX programme). She spent seven years in industry before returning to academia: at AstraZeneca she led model-informed drug development in cardiovascular, renal, and metabolic programmes and was the MASH disease area lead, and at InSilicoTrials she was Scientific Director, combining pharmacometrics and AI.

She chairs the AI/ML Special Interest Group of the International Society of Pharmacometrics (ISoP), is on the editorial board of *CPT: Pharmacometrics & Systems Pharmacology*, and received the Hans-Günter-Schäfer Award in 2020.

## Teaching

We teach pharmacometrics and modelling at the University of Copenhagen, including *Basic Pharmacology* (Bachelor, medicinal chemistry) and the elective *Pharmacometrics* course (Master, Pharmacy). We supervise Master's projects in modelling and machine learning for pharmaceutical science.

## Join us

Fully funded PhD and postdoc positions are advertised at [employment.ku.dk](https://employment.ku.dk). We also welcome inquiries from MSc, exchange, and scholarship students. The group is interdisciplinary, and we value backgrounds in mathematics, pharmacy, data science, and the life sciences.
